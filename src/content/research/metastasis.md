title: Events regulating breast cancer metastasis
image: ./metastasis.jpg
summary: Breast cancer commonly spreads to the lymph node, brain, bone, and lungs. This metastatic spread ultimately causes patient mortality and is a major focus of the lab.

Our research into mouse models of breast cancer has allowed us to use bioinformatics to make predictions about which genetic pathways are required for the development and progression of breast cancer. Testing these predictions through interbreeding genetic strains has identified a number of pathways that are crucial for the progression of breast cancer to metastatic disease.

For instance, we predicted a role for the E2F transcription factors in various models and observed that ablation of E2F1 or E2F2 resulted in a vast reduction in metastasis (MCB 2014 and Oncogene 2015). By integrating whole-genome sequencing with gene expression analysis, we have identified a number of mechanisms that regulate metastasis and are currently exploring them in engineered mouse models, breast cancer cell lines, and human metastatic genomic data.
